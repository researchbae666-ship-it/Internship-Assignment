# RefillLoop 30-day campaign strategy

## Campaign premise and positioning

RefillLoop is a fictional proposed modular reusable-hydration concept. The
campaign positions it as a routine-design idea for people who move between a
commute, desk, training, and travel. The proposed differentiation is not a
material, health, or environmental-performance claim: it is the combination of
modular routine framing, practical habit education, workplace activation, and
feedback-led concept validation.

The campaign has five planned phases: Days 1–7 tease the everyday friction;
Days 8–12 reveal the concept; Days 13–18 explain intended use and care
assumptions; Days 19–22 test message routes; and Days 23–30 invite early-access
interest and capture learning for a future real launch.

## Audience and targeting assumptions

- **Maya, hybrid commuter (24–38):** wants fewer carry decisions between work
  locations; test creative in commuting, productivity, and hybrid-work contexts.
- **Sam, active routine builder (22–35):** wants portable routine cues around
  training and work; test creative in training, wellbeing-content, and reusable
  product contexts.
- **Aisha, workplace wellbeing lead:** needs practical internal activations;
  use policy-compliant LinkedIn context/job-function tests for people
  operations, workplace experience, and facilities roles.

These are planning personas, not researched customer segments. Targeting must
be reviewed against each platform's current policy before any future launch.

## Channel roles

- **Instagram:** daily Reels, carousels, and Stories provide visual routine
  prompts, conversation starters, saves, and community feedback.
- **LinkedIn:** selected professional-context days provide thought leadership,
  B2B use cases, workplace toolkits, polls, and implementation storytelling.
- **YouTube:** Shorts create discovery and simple cues; long-form videos explain
  the fictional concept, practical demonstrations, and proposed workplace
  toolkit.

## Measurement, tests, and optimization

Primary measures are platform-native reach, video attention, saves/replies,
qualified UTM clicks, consent-aware interest-form completions, and feedback
themes. No achieved result is implied. Planned A/B tests compare utility-first
versus lifestyle-first creative, “join interest list” versus “explore the
concept” CTA, a commuter versus desk-day hook, and document versus text-post
presentation. Hold a decision log: hypothesis, audience, creative, primary KPI,
delivery context, result, and next action.

Optimization rules: do not declare a winner from a single post; pause only a
paid variant after delivery is sufficient for its pre-agreed test threshold;
move budget in small documented increments; and preserve an untreated control
creative where feasible. Risks include unclear fictional-product disclosure,
unsupported claims, comment moderation delays, consent issues, creative
production slippage, and platform-delivery volatility. Each requires a named
owner and a fallback creative or response path.

## Budget model

The illustrative media-only budget is USD 3,000: Instagram USD 1,350, LinkedIn
USD 1,050, and YouTube USD 600. This is a proposed allocation, not spend. It
excludes production, web, agency, tax, and platform fees. Actual bids, CPM,
conversion rate, and cost per interest must be measured only during a
authorized live campaign.

## Copy variations

1. **Utility:** “Your day changes. Your refill routine can have a plan.” CTA:
   “Explore the concept.”
2. **Routine:** “One small cue can make the next refill easier to remember.”
   CTA: “Try a habit stack.”
3. **Professional:** “Workplace wellbeing ideas need an owner, a cue, and a
   way to learn.” CTA: “Save the activation template.”
4. **Feedback:** “What would make a reusable routine easier to repeat?” CTA:
   “Share one barrier.”
