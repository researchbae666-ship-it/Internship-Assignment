# Digital Marketing research plan

## Proposed coherent market context

The proposed consumer sector is **reusable hydration products**. It supports a
comparable consumer-brand audit and a credible sustainability/wellness campaign
context without claiming a relationship with any selected brand.

### Task 2 proposed competitors

| Proposed brand | Why it is a reasonable candidate | Collection status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stanley 1913 | Consumer drinkware brand with public channels likely relevant to reusable hydration positioning | Not collected |
| Hydro Flask | Consumer insulated-bottle brand likely comparable on product, lifestyle, and sustainability messaging | Not collected |
| Owala | Consumer hydration brand likely comparable on product design and social-led positioning | Not collected |

These selections are hypotheses for approval. Their current platform presence,
posting cadence, visible engagement, and positioning must be verified from
public sources before appearing as factual findings.

### Task 3 proposed campaign context

Use **RefillLoop**, a clearly fictional proposed modular hydration product, as
the campaign subject: a reusable bottle system with replaceable insulated
components and a refill habit-tracking companion. It is a planning concept, not
an existing company or campaign. The 30-day plan will label all budgets,
audiences, KPIs, assets, and performance values as assumptions or targets.

### Task 4 proposed website context

Subject to public-access verification, evaluate **LARQ
(`https://www.livelarq.com/`)** as a consumer hydration-technology company
website. The future report will only assert details observed on the public site
on a recorded date. If inaccessible or unsuitable for the startup criterion, a
replacement will be selected through the same documented rationale before
research begins.

## Public research method

1. Create a source-registry entry before using a source.
2. Use official brand websites and public Instagram, LinkedIn, and YouTube
   pages/posts where accessible. Record URL, collection time, publication date,
   and exactly visible fields.
3. Use a stated, shared observation window (proposed: the most recent 28
   calendar days available at collection) for Task 2. Do not mix windows.
4. Calculate only with captured public inputs. A visible-interaction rate is an
   estimate, never first-party engagement analytics. Mark unavailable fields
   unavailable.
5. For Task 4, record observable title/meta/heading/URL/internal-link and
   technical signals only. Do not claim analytics, rankings, search volume,
   conversions, ad-account data, or email-platform access.
6. Use public search-result features and site content for keyword ideas. Treat
   volumes, conversion rates, CAC, revenue, and ROAS as labeled model inputs
   unless a reliable public source supports a distinct fact.
7. Conduct a citation, calculation, and claim-label review before drafting each
   report.
